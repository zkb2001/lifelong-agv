ABSTRACT 
This project report presents the development of an Automated Greater Bay Area (GBA) 
Vehicle License Plate Recognition System aimed at addressing the increasing need for 
efficient vehicle management in public places such as parking lots, Electronic Toll 
Collection, and intelligent transportation. With the influx of vehicles from Macau and 
Hong Kong into the GBA, the demand for advanced vehicle management solution has 
become significant. Traditional manual methods of vehicle identification are no longer 
viable due to the high volume of vehicles and the diversity of license plates. This report 
proposed a system utilizing efficient image processing and machine learning algorithms 
to accurately and efficiently recognize and process vehicle license plates from Macau, 
Hong Kong, and the broader Guangdong Province. The system is designed to be 
integrated with the existing algorithms of parking lot infrastructures, providing the 
improvements to enhance operations efficiency, reduce human error, and improve the 
intelligence of parking facilities. Presented in this report are the system’s design, 
development process, challenges encountered, and solutions applied to overcome them. 
It also outlines a comprehensive evaluation of the system’s performance, demonstrating 
the effectiveness and potential future expectations for vehicle management in the GBA 
region.

INTRODUCTION 
Automatic License Plate Recognition (ALPR) is an important research for intelligent 
transportation systems which can be utilized in parking lot management, vehicle 
identification and traffic surveillance by extracting and recognizing the characters on 
license plates (LPs) from image processing and pattern recognition technologies 
(Examples shown in Figure 1). In recent years, machine learning methods have become 
mainstream in license plate recognition tasks. Although different deep learning 
methods have been applied in the market, there are still many problems that need to be 
optimized and solved such as poor lighting conditions, variability of license plates 
characters (including special characters), blurry images, and weather conditions (shown 
in Figure 2). Conventional approaches to license plate recognition (LPR) typically rely 
on methods based on segmentation, involving the detection of individual characters 
followed by their recognition. These approaches require precise segmentation of the 
characters. At the same time, errors in this segmentation process can result in incorrect 
identification of license plates, even with a reliable character recognition system in 
place. Nevertheless, in practical settings, factors such as blurred images and 
environmental conditions compromise the precision of character segmentation. 
Furthermore, the division of character detection and recognition into separate processes 
lead to extra computational expenses that can impact the efficiency of the algorithm. A 
solid ALPR system must effectively handle diverse environmental conditions while 
ensuring high accuracy and perform reliably under real-world circumstances.

Related Work 
Generally, the common task on LPR can be divided into two parts: detecting the LPs 
and then recognizing the characters on them. This section will first explore the 
techniques used for LP detection and recognition, as well as a summary of the relevant 
research in this area. Given that our primary concern is license plate detection and 
recognition (LPDR), we exclude research related to the detection of vehicles.  
1. License Plate Detection 
In traditional approaches, a LP detection algorithm typically involves a series of steps 
designed to locate and identify the license plate within in image which is relies on 
specific features like edge, color, texture, and patterns. However, these manually 
defined features often struggle with complex backgrounds. In contrast, Convolutional 
Neural Networks (CNNs) have become the leading technique in object detection in 
recent years. With sufficient training data, CNNs are capable of capturing and 
representing features with superior performance, delivering significantly better results 
than traditional methods that depend on predefined features. In this section, we 
concentrate on detection methods by using CNNs that can be categorized into two main 
types: two-stage LP detection and one-stage LP detection.  
1.1 Two-stage LP detection 
Region-based Convolutional Neural Networks, also known as R-CNNs [4], are at the 
pioneer of applying deep learning models to detecting objects in two-stage. R-CNNs 
begin by identifying various potential regions within an image, such as using anchor 
boxes. And then classify their categories and determine bounding box (for instance, 
through offsets) based on a set of predefined features. Nevertheless, two-stage detection

majority of LP detection techniques prefer the one-stage detection framework due to its 
higher efficiency even if two-stage detection methods have higher accuracy. 
1.2 One-stage LP detection 
Popular one-stage LP detection methods, such as YOLO (You Only Look Once) series 
and SSD [5] (Single Shot Multibox Detector), are real-time object detection algorithms 
used in many commercial products. YOLO predicts class possibility and bounding box 
in a single shot which significantly improve the speed in the detection phase. The 
original YOLO object detector was first released in 2016, and the new architecture was 
significantly faster than many object detectors.  
SSD is a popular detector that can predict multiple classes. It operates by segmenting 
the space for potential bounding boxes into a grid of default boxes, varying by aspect 
ratio and scale across different locations on the feature map through a singular deep 
neural network. However, SSD can struggle with small objects within an image due to 
its use of fixed default boxes especially those objects take large part of them. The 
performance of SSD can be sensitive to the aspect ratios and scales of the objects since 
it relies on predefined defaulted boxes.  
Compared to YOLO series, especially the later versions like YOLOv5 [6], and 
YOLOv7 [7], which are generally optimized for speed. While SSD is also designed for 
the real-time processing, YOLO’s newer architecture allows it to process image more 
quickly in some implementations. Detecting small objects within images presents 
challenges for both SSD and YOLO models, but the problem can be more obvious with 
SSD. The grid-based system employed by YOLO can handle a more comprehensive 
understanding of the entire image’s context, potentially improving the detection of

2. License Plate Recognition 
License Plate Recognition (LPR) algorithms can be broadly categorized into two types 
based on their approach to identifying and reading characters and numbers on a given 
LP: segmentation-based recognition and segmentation-free recognition.  
2.1 Segmentation-based Recognition 
The process of segmentation-based LPR typically involves three main steps: Plate 
detection, character segmentation, and character recognition. The first step is to locate 
the license plate within the image by using various image processing techniques such 
as edge detection, morphological operations, combining projections, and other hand-
crafted algorithms. Then the plates will be segmented into individual characters by 
algorithms generally from two methods: The first approach utilizes connected 
component methods that create cohesive regions from images that have been converted 
to binary, treating each region as an individual character. And the second approach 
employs projection methods to isolate each character by identifying the upper and lower 
edges derived from the horizontal projection of binary images [8][9][10][11][12]. 
Finally, the areas are identified individually through the use of optical character 
recognition (OCR) methods [13][14]. 
However, these methods suffer from segmentation errors cause the segmentation 
quality can be highly affected by the input image noise, blur, deformations, or low

resolution. In this report, segmentation-free recognition methods are chosen to 
concentrate on. 
2.2 Segmentation-free Recognition 
Segmentation-free LP algorithms do not rely on the individual segmentation of 
characters on the license plate. Instead, this type of approach directly performs LP 
recognition in a single step. The process of segmentation-free LPR include more 
complex model like CNNs that are capable of understanding the plate directly from 
detected plate region. Techniques like Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) [15] or 
Transformer [16] model can process the entire plate image and output the plate’s text 
as a sequence of characters. In TE2E [17], Li et al. proposed a bidirectional RNNs 
(BRNNs) with connectionist temporal classification (CTC) loss [33] to process LP 
features produced by RPN. Similarly, Wang et al. [18] proposed a convolutional RNN 
(CRNN) followed by the CTC for character recognition. Generally, CNNs based 
structures are more efficient than the RNNs based methods when they have the same 
number of parameters in the LPR. In this report, a proposed method called XRNet is 
created and has high recognition performance by using CNNs.

1. ALPR System Design 
This section delineates the systematical design implemented in the development of the 
ALPR system. The methodology typically contains image acquisition, preprocessing, 
license plate detection, character recognition, and post-processing stages, integrated 
with advanced computer vision and machine learning algorithms. The primary goal of 
this research is to present a method for detecting and recognizing license plate in real-
time with accuracy. In contrast to segmentation-based approaches which require the 
segmentation of characters before their recognition, the proposed method employ the 
cascade CNNs with Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) loss [2] for training, 
eliminating the requirement to mark the character positions on the license plate. The 
proposed infrastructure was roughly divided into two parts shown in Figure 3, the 
detection phase is based on YOLOv7 with some optimized components and the 
recognition phase is built on the proposed XRNet relying on the insight of SCR-Net

2. License Plate Detection 
The proposed XRNet adopts a high performance one-stage detector implemented based

Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) is adapted to serve as the fusion network by 
integrating the features from the backbone network.

2.3 Fusion Network 
The Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) [20] in fusion network operates by incrementally 
decreasing the input image's spatial resolution while simultaneously increasing the 
number of feature channels. This process generates feature maps adept at identifying 
objects across a variety of scales and resolutions. Two phases are processed on this 
architecture in the fusion network. Initially, bilinear upsampling is applied to the 
features with lower resolution, enlarging them by a factor of 2 to align their sizes. 
Subsequently, the features with higher resolution passing through Batch Normalization 
(BN) and a Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU). Following this, the two sets of feature maps 
are combined and then passed through a sequence consisting of a 1×1 stride, 
convolution, BN, and ReLU.

3.3 Special Case Handling 
To handle the special characters in ALPR due to the wide variety of license plate 
formats, symbols, and characters across different regions and countries. Some strategies 
are adopted as follows: 
• Extended special character dataset: Use data augmentation techniques by 
Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) such as StyleText to artificially expand 
the training dataset with images that include special characters can improve the 
model’s robustness. 
• Pre-processing: Apply image preprocessing techniques such as denoising and 
contrast adjustment to improve the clarity of characters on the license plate. In 
addition, geometric transformations are utilized to rectify the perspective of 
license plates in images, ensuring that the characters are aligned for easier 
recognition.

• Post-processing: Post-processing rules are implemented based on known license 
plate formats and conventions to validate and correct recognized outputs by the

error correction algorithms are adopted to identify and rectify common 
recognition mistakes, especially for characters that are easily confused with 
special symbols.

ALPR systems. These challenges include varying line alignments, different character 
sizes, and spacing. After the two-line LP is detected by the proposed detection model, 
the LP is divided by a geometric transformation into two parts in a certain ratio of 5:7. 
And two parts of LP is aligned into one longer license plate as an input for the LP 
recognition phase.

EXPERIMENTS 
To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed model, the experiments on different 
dataset are conducted for both LP detection and recognition. In this section, the datasets 
and settings for training and testing will be introduced first. Then the evaluation with 
implementation details of the proposed methods compared to other popular methods

1. Datasets 
The proposed ALPR models are evaluated on three datasets, i.e. CCPD, HMLPD, and 
HMLPD-Mixed. 
1.1 CCPD dataset 
CCPD stands for Chinese City Parking Datasets which is an extensive publicly 
accessible LP dataset with over 250k unique car images in proving annotations for the 
vertices’ locations. Each image in the dataset has dimensions of 720 pixels in width, 
1160 pixels in height, and 3 color channels. This resolution ensures that the license 
plates within each image are clear and readable. On average, the size of each file is 
approximately 200 kilobytes, cumulatively amounting to more than 48 gigabytes for 
the entire dataset. Except the large amount of data contains in the dataset, one of the 
advantages of the dataset is that it has images under diverse illuminations, environments 
in different weather, and different angle of views to ensure distinct evaluation 
conditions.  
1.2 HMLPD and HMLPD-Mixed dataset

3. Implementation Details 
3.1 LP Detection Training 
The detection model is trained by 100 epochs with the batch size of 128 on the GPU. 
The adaptive learning rate strategy was applied in the training with initially 0.01 and 
cut it by a factor of 0.5 every 20 epochs to improve training efficiency and model 
performance. For optimization, we chose the SGD optimizer, setting a weight decay of 
0.0005 and a momentum value of 0.9. The model processes all input images after 
resizing them to a uniform width of 160 pixels, maintaining their original aspect ratios. 
To enhance the robustness of the model, the vertex-based data augmentation techniques 
is incorporated, including random flipping and affine transformation implemented by 
OpenCV library with rotating, scaling, and fuzzy. 
3.2 LP Recognition Training 
The recognition model is trained with ‘Adam’ optimizer with a batch size of 64, an 
initial learning rate of 0.001, and a gradient noise scale of 0.001. The learning rate is 
reduced by a factor of 10 every 10 epochs, with a total training for 200 epochs. To 
enhance the model’s robustness, some data augmentation techniques are applied before 
training phase such as random affine transformations, which include horizonal rotations, 
scaling, shifts, and resizes. The selection of all other hyper-parameters was selected 
based on cross-validation on the target dataset.

4.1 YOLOv3, YOLOv5, Faster R-CNN Overview 
4.1.1 Yolov3 Overview:  
YOLOv3 is a widely recognized object detection model known for its speed and 
accuracy. It’s the third version of the YOLO series, developed by Joseph Redmon et al. 
The model’s ability to perform real-time object detection makes it highly relevant for 
projects that require fast and efficient detection tasks, including those in surveillance, 
autonomous driving, and other various real-time monitoring systems. 
Key Features: 
• Speed and Real-Time Processing: One of the standout features of YOLOv3 is 
its capability to process images in real-time, making it incredibly fast compared 
to some of its counterparts. This is achieved through a single neural network 
that predicts bounding boxes and class probabilities directly from full images in 
one evaluation. 
• Darknet-53 [24] Backbone: YOLOv3 uses Darknet-53 as its feature extractor, 
which is a convolutional neural network with 53 convolutional layers,

4.1.2 YOLOv5 Overview 
Although YOLOv5 is not an official release from the original YOLO developers, it is 
recognized for its improvements in accuracy and speed over previous version. YOLOv5 
builds upon the strengths of YOLOv3 while addressing some of its limitations. The 
enhancements in speed and accuracy make YOLOv5 a powerful baseline, particularly 
in applications where real-time processing and high accuracy are significant.  
Key features: 
• Enhanced Speed and Accuracy: YOLOv5 provides significant improvements in 
detection speed and accuracy compared to YOLOv3, making it more effective 
for real-time applications. 
• Simplified Model Structure: Features a more streamlined and simplified 
architecture, making it easier to train and deploy even on devices with limited 
computational power. 
• Automatic Optimization: Includes automatic learning rate scheduling and 
model pruning, which optimize the model’s performance without extensive 
manual tuning. 
4.1.3 Faster R-CNN Overview 
Faster R-CNN is an advanced object detector developed by Kaiming He et al. which 
integrates the region proposal process directly into the network. It significantly 
increasing the speed over its predecessor, Fast R-CNN by using a Region Proposal 
Network (RPN) to generate object proposals in a nearly cost-free manner as part of the

networks. While Faster R-CNN significantly improved the speed of region-based object 
detection methods, the YOLO series, particularly YOLOv5 and YOLOv7, are 
optimized for real-time detection and are generally faster in processing images. Faster 
R-CNN is known for its high precision and accuracy, particularly in handling complex 
scenes with small or overlapping objects. Faster R-CNN's dual-component structure 
(RPN and detection network) can make it more complex and computationally intensive 
to train compared to the more straightforward single-network architecture of YOLO 
models which traded some precision for speed. However, the YOLO series, especially 
in its later versions, offers outstanding advantages for scenarios requiring fast and 
efficient real-time object detection, with considerations around the balance of speed and 
accuracy. 
Key Features: 
• Region Proposal Network: The RPN in Faster R-CNN allows for the generation 
of high-quality object proposals, which are used by the detection network for 
accurate object detection. 
• Anchor Boxes: It utilizes anchor boxes with varying scales and aspect ratios, 
improving the model's ability to handle objects of different sizes and shapes. 
• End-to-End Training: The model can be trained end-to-end, allowing for the 
RPN and the detection network to benefit from shared convolutional features, 
leading to more efficient training and better performance. 
• Feature Sharing: The sharing of convolutional features between the RPN and 
the detection network reduces the computational load and enhances the speed 
of the model.

5.1 CRNN and Bi-LSTM Overview 
5.1.1 CRNN Overview 
The CRNN method merges the capabilities of CNNs and Recurrent Neural Networks 
(RNNs) to create a powerful model for sequence recognition tasks, such as text 
recognition in images. This model stands out for its proficiency in processing images 


===== PAGE 33 =====
Your Project Title 
33 of 43 
 
15 September 2026 
and predict character sequences without the need for explicit segmentation. This makes 
it particularly relevant for applications like LP recognition, where it can directly 
interpret the characters on a LP from the image data.  
Key Features: 
• Hybrid Architecture: Combines CNN layers for feature extraction from images 
with RNN layers for sequence modeling, capturing spatial hierarchies and 
dependencies between characters in sequences. 
• End-to-End Trainability: CRNN is designed for end-to-end training, that is the 
model’s learning from raw image data to sequence output to enhance its 
efficiency and overall performance. 
• CTC Decoding: Employs CTC for decoding the character sequences from the 
RNN outputs, enabling the model to handle sequences of variable lengths and 
to predict sequences without segmentation. 
CRNN’s integration of CNN and RNN architectures allows for effective handling of 
the complex patterns and sequences in LP characters, making it a robust baseline for 
LP recognition projects. 
5.1.2 Bi-LSTM Overview 
The Bi-LSTM (Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory) model from Zou et al. 
enhances traditional LSTM networks by processing data in both forward and reverse 
directions, offering a comprehensive mechanism for tasks involving sequential data, 
such as image-based text recognition. Its dual-direction processing capability is 
especially beneficial for applications like LP recognition, enabling the model to capture 
contextual information from both preceding and succeeding characters in a LP sequence. 


===== PAGE 34 =====
Your Project Title 
34 of 43 
 
15 September 2026 
 
Key Attributes: 
• Bidirectional Processing: Bi-LSTM layers analyze sequences from both ends, 
forward and backward, providing a richer context and understanding of the 
sequence’s dynamics, which is crucial for accurately recognizing the order and 
relationship of characters in sequences on LPs. 
• Enhanced Memory Capabilities: Bi-LSTM extends the ability to remember 
long-term dependencies by maintaining two separate memory paths for both 
directions of the sequence that can improve the model’s ability to preserve 
relevant information across long sequences. 
• One-Dimensional (1D) attention: 1D attention is implemented to improve the 
key character features and minimize the irrelevant character features in LPs, 
which can effectively extract features and improve the accuracy of LP 
recognition. 
In summary, the BiLSTM architecture improves contextual comprehension via 
bidirectional analysis of sequences, potentially yielding enhanced performance in 
scenarios where an exhaustive understanding of context is significant. In contrast, the 
CRNN framework engages in unidirectional sequence processing and incorporates 
convolutional layers designed for the extraction of spatial features that is beneficial for 
image processing tasks such as LP Recognition, in which initial extraction of spatial 
attributes is critical. Conversely, BiLSTM configurations may require the integration 
of additional mechanisms or layers to effectively perform feature extraction from image 
data.

system. The research and findings indicate that the light-weight system could achieve
